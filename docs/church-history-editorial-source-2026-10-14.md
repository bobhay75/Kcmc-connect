## Details transcribed for reference

- Hillbilly Bowl opened August 17, 1962; first church services were held there the following week.
- The Joplin District purchased the church lot for $5,000 and asked retired pastor John Wesley McKinney to organize the congregation.
- December 9: twenty-two members were accepted. The year is implied by the preceding 1962 chronology, not explicitly printed beside that date.
- January 1963: Lee Whiteside was assigned as pastor and helped oversee groundbreaking, consecration and construction.
- Dick and Fern Sherer joined in March 1963.
- Official charter: June 12, 1963; 53 members.
- Sanctuary expansion: 1972.
- Membership recorded by Fern: 750 by 1976.
- Addition: 8,700 square feet of offices and Sunday school rooms in 1982; 864 members at Palm Sunday dedication.
- Community activities recalled: Tri-Lakes Chorale, Well Baby Clinic, Hughes Memorial Band, Kimberling Klub, Red Cross Bloodmobile, driver's-license testing, Hospice, Young Life and County Extension Homemakers.
- Virginia “Mom” Sherer helped begin the Women's Society of Christian Service. Ruth Waltz and Vera Lou Biggerstaff co-signed the organ note; Ray Gossett paid the interest. Women raised money through bake sales, dinners and other projects, later provided handbells and supported missions/local charities.
- Pastor Leland Loy is described as a retired Army chaplain who encouraged new ideas.
- “Our congregation seems to always be changing” and “Perhaps we can explore new ways of doing things” are quotations the supplied summary attributes to Fern.

## Regional-history material held for research

Page one connects this area to a Wilderness Road traveled between northern Arkansas, southern Missouri and Springfield; a White River crossing; and Radical community/campground where travelers waited for high water to fall. It says Radical disappeared beneath Table Rock Lake. Page two interprets the church as continuing this pattern of gathering along a journey. These claims have not been independently checked in this task and are omitted from the public adaptation pending research.

Page three's closing argument says the founders responded to a changing community and asks today's congregation to have the courage to do the same. Preserve this as the supplied summary's interpretation rather than a separately sourced historical fact.

The source photographs are not included in the app or public repository. In 49.jpg an unrelated staff timesheet is visible behind the history page; no timesheet details are transcribed, displayed or used for time accounting.
